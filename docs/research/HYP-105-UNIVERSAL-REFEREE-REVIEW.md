## 2. External theorem is real, but its constants are NOT 1

The actual load-bearing external statement is standard **Simonovits balanced graph even-cycle supersaturation** for six-cycles:
\[
   e(G)\ge C_{\rm graph}v(G)^{4/3}
   \quad\Rightarrow\quad
   \# C_6(G)\ge c_{\rm graph}(e(G)/v(G))^6 ,
\]
where absolute positive \(C_{\rm graph},c_{\rm graph}\) exist. A published independent primary source explicitly states it with general \(C_{2k}\) and includes its graph \(r=2\) case:
Tao Jiang and Liana Yepremyan, *Supersaturation of even linear cycles in linear hypergraphs*, **Combinatorics, Probability and Computing** 29(5) (2020), 698–721,
DOI [10.1017/S0963548320000206](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548320000206),
[Cambridge abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/combinatorics-probability-and-computing/article/abs/supersaturation-of-even-linear-cycles-in-linear-hypergraphs/240493C1F42618819DA9B16CE7F3E4BD).
The paper attributes the graph case to Simonovits; the notation here accommodates simple unoriented unrooted six-cycles by adjusting the constants. Its premise is on one **ordinary balanced chunk subgraph** F, not on a conjectural unbalanced graph supersaturation bound.

The integer arithmetic tests instantiate **formal placeholder C=1,c=1 and two other normalized pairs** solely to falsify algebraic implications. It would be incorrect to claim that the external theorem's actual valid constants are C=c=1 or that its explicit s0 is numerically known. The valid quantitative theorem is \(\exists C,c>0\), choose any valid normalized pair, then define positive explicit \(\rho,\delta,s_0\).
