## 1. The Fundamental Analytical Reduction

Let $H'$ be the spectrally pruned rectangle-diagonal graph on $[a] \times [a]$ retaining $m' = (1 - o(1)) m$ edges ($m = 2 V \Delta$). By the stationary walk entropy theorem ([PR #394](HYP-105-ISSUE230-PROJECTION-CLOSURE.md)), the number of proper oriented 3-paths $P$ with complete physical endpoints satisfies:
$$P \ge (16 - o(1)) s^6.$$

Every such path $\gamma = (v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3)$ has physical endpoints $f(q) = \{x_0, x_3\}$ and $g(r) = \{y_0, y_3\}$ with $q \in P, r \in L$. The path partition is exact and disjoint:
$$P = P_{\text{missing}} + P_{\text{inc}} + B(f, g) + 2 BB_{\text{sync}}(H').$$

1. **Missing Endpoints:** In the minimal physical alphabet, $t = \binom{a}{2} - V < a$. Pruning coordinates with missing degree $>\kappa$ ensures $P_{\text{missing}} = o(s^6)$.
2. **Incident Endpoints ($P_{\text{inc}}$):** $q \sim r$ in $W(3,s)$. By the Mixed-Book Theorem:
   $$P_{\text{inc}} \le 24 T M_2(G) + \sqrt{192 m D6(H')}.$$
   Because $24 T M_2(G) = O(s^{28/5}) = o(s^6)$, if $P_{\text{inc}} \ge \eta s^6$, then:
   $$D6(H') \ge \frac{(\eta - o(1))^2 s^{12}}{192 m} = \Omega(\eta^2 s^8) \gg \Omega(s^6).$$
   Therefore, incident paths cannot absorb the path budget without forcing super-quartic motif growth ($s^8$).
3. **Nonincident Unblocked Paths:** $q \not\sim r$ and no anchor collisions. The unique GQ projection $q \sim l \sim p \sim r$ yields an accepted, distinct 6-set of incidences in the synchronized $BB$-overlap family ($BBo$). Each accepted motif is generated by exactly 2 oriented paths (reversal invariance):
   $$BB_{\text{sync}}(H') = \frac{1}{2} P_{\text{unblocked}} \le S(f, g).$$
4. **Anchor-Blocked Defect ($B(f, g)$):** $q \not\sim r$ and either $f(p) \cap \{x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3\} \neq \emptyset$ or $g(l) \cap \{y_0, y_1, y_2, y_3\} \neq \emptyset$.

Combining these identities yields the universal master reduction:
$$\boxed{(16 - o(1)) s^6 \le B(f, g) + 2 S(f, g) + \sqrt{192 m S(f, g)}}.$$

## 2. Discrete Path Congestion and Endpoint Structure

Let $B(f, g) = E(f, g) + I(f, g)$, where $E$ counts endpoint collisions and $I$ counts internal-only collisions on endpoint-good pairs.

### Theorem 1 (Neighborhood Edge Representation)
For any fixed nonincident pair $(q, r) \in P \times L$ and any fixed orientation $v_0 = (x_0, y_0)$, $v_3 = (x_3, y_3)$:
1. The intermediate incidences $(p_1, l_1)$ and $(p_3, l_3)$ uniquely determine the middle vertex coordinates:
   $$f(p_2) = \{x_1, x_2\}, \qquad g(l_2) = \{y_1, y_2\}.$$
   By global injectivity of $f$ and $g$, there is at most **one** choice for $p_2 \in P$ and at most **one** choice for $l_2 \in L$.
2. The number of length-3 paths in $H'$ between $v_0$ and $v_3$ is exactly:
   $$N_{v_0, v_3} = e_{H'}(N_{H'}(v_0), N_{H'}(v_3)) \le \min(\deg_{H'}(v_0), \deg_{H'}(v_3)) \cdot 2(s+1) \le 4(s+1)^2.$$
3. The set of endpoint-bad pairs $U = B_L \cup B_R$ satisfies:
   $$|U| \le 4 s C_s = O(s^5).$$
   Since the total number of nonincident pairs is $V_{\text{noninc}} = s(s+1)^2(s^2+1) = (1 + o(1)) s^6$, the fraction of endpoint-bad pairs is:
   $$\frac{|U|}{V_{\text{noninc}}} = O\left(\frac{1}{s}\right) \xrightarrow{s \to \infty} 0.$$
